Build log · this site

How this site was built

With AI, without writing code, in a field I had never worked in. Here is who did what, what I decided and what it took.

01 The goal

Clear in three seconds. True in every line.

A site anyone understands at a glance, in English and Polish, with the Polish version written rather than translated. Every claim backed by my resume or my own documents. No personal details beyond my name, city, email and LinkedIn. And an owner, me, who is not a developer.

02 Who did what

I set the direction. AI did the build.

My part

  • The goal, the requirements and the limits.
  • The facts and the content.
  • Choosing between proposals, and the final word on quality.
  • Asking for every claim to be checked against my documents.

The AI's part

  • Turning my guidelines into a written brief.
  • Drafting the designs and the text.
  • Writing all of the code.
  • Running the checks and keeping a log of every decision.

03 My decisions

What I turned down, and why.

  1. The first three design directions

    They looked like a business card, not a website.

  2. The proposed red accent

    I kept the color from my resume, so the site and the resume look like one set.

  3. A headline that credited me with 20 hours a month

    The automation saves those hours. I found errors in its process before it was built, and the site says exactly that.

  4. The clearest headline

    “I catch process errors before they get built” promises more than my experience. The past tense tells what happened.

  5. Anything I could not back up

    Tools I don't know well enough came off the list, and the case study is marked as an exercise, not client work.

04 The result

Under two days, no extra cost.

  • Under two days

    brief to publication · about 47 h, in sessions

  • No extra cost

    beyond the Claude subscription I already use · free hosting

  • 4 headline versions

    plus 3 rejected design directions · 5 highlighter attempts

→ What I learned

Two things I'm keeping.

Every claim needs checking against a source: AI writes convincingly even when it gets a detail wrong.

Precise requirements save rework: the more exactly I described what I wanted, the fewer rounds it took.
